# IFRS and ISA study notes
These are short plain-language summaries written for study. They are not the official text. Always check the official standard before relying on any point.

## IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements: going concern and current or non-current classification
Management must assess whether the entity can continue as a going concern. The assessment looks at least twelve months ahead from the end of the reporting period. If there are material uncertainties that cast significant doubt on the entity's ability to continue, they must be disclosed. Assets and liabilities are shown as current or non-current based on when they are expected to be realised or settled. Weak liquidity, losses and loan problems are typical warning signs.

## IAS 2 Inventories: measurement and net realisable value
Inventories are measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value. Cost includes purchase cost, conversion costs and other costs of bringing items to their present location and condition. The cost formulas allowed are first-in first-out and weighted average; last-in first-out is not allowed. Inventory is written down to net realisable value when it is damaged, obsolete, or when selling prices fall below cost. Common audit work includes attending the stock count, testing the costing and reviewing slow-moving items.

## IAS 16 Property, Plant and Equipment: depreciation and useful life
Property, plant and equipment is recognised at cost and depreciated systematically over its useful life from when it is available for use. The useful life and residual value are reviewed at least at each financial year-end. An entity may use the cost model or the revaluation model for a whole class of assets. Audit work often covers additions, disposals, the depreciation calculation and the existence of the assets. Accumulated depreciation reduces the carrying amount.

## IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers: recognition and cut-off
Revenue is recognised when, or as, control of the promised goods or services passes to the customer, at the amount the entity expects to be entitled to. The model has five steps: identify the contract, identify the performance obligations, determine the transaction price, allocate the price, and recognise revenue. Cut-off is a common risk because sales near the period end can be recorded in the wrong period. Auditors compare delivery documents and invoices around the year-end with the dates recorded.

## IFRS 9 Financial Instruments: impairment of trade receivables
Trade receivables are assessed for expected credit losses. A simplified approach lets an entity measure the loss allowance at lifetime expected credit losses, often using a provision matrix based on ageing and past experience. Auditors review the ageing of receivables, later cash receipts and the reasonableness of the allowance. Changes in receivables turnover can signal collection problems.

## IFRS 16 Leases: rent and right-of-use assets
For most leases a lessee recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability instead of expensing the rent in full. There are exemptions for short-term leases and leases of low-value assets, where payments can be expensed. Rent expense in a trial balance may therefore need review to see whether a lease should appear on the balance sheet.

## IAS 37 Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets
A provision is recognised when there is a present obligation from a past event, an outflow of resources is probable, and the amount can be estimated reliably. A contingent liability is disclosed, not recognised, unless the chance of an outflow is remote. Auditors ask about legal cases, claims and guarantees.

## IAS 10 Events after the Reporting Period
Events after the reporting date that give evidence of conditions that existed at the reporting date are adjusting events and change the figures. Events that arise after the reporting date are non-adjusting and are disclosed if material. Auditors review the period up to the date of their report.

## ISA 240 The Auditor's Responsibilities Relating to Fraud
The auditor seeks reasonable assurance that the financial statements are free from material misstatement, whether caused by fraud or error. Management override of controls is always treated as a risk. Required procedures include testing the appropriateness of journal entries and other adjustments, reviewing accounting estimates for bias, and understanding the business reason for significant unusual transactions. Journal entries that are manual, round, posted at unusual times or to unusual accounts deserve attention. An exception found by a test is something to investigate and is not proof of fraud.

## ISA 315 and ISA 330 Risk assessment and responses to assessed risks
The auditor identifies and assesses the risks of material misstatement and then designs audit procedures that respond to those risks. Significant accounts and disclosures are those where a material misstatement is reasonably possible. A higher assessed risk calls for more persuasive audit evidence.

## ISA 320 Materiality in planning and performing an audit
The auditor sets an overall materiality for the financial statements and a lower performance materiality to reduce the risk that uncorrected and undetected misstatements add up to more than materiality. Setting materiality involves professional judgement. Common benchmarks include revenue, total assets, profit before tax and equity; the choice depends on the entity and the users of its statements.

## ISA 500, ISA 505 and ISA 530 Audit evidence, external confirmations and sampling
Audit evidence must be sufficient and appropriate. Evidence is generally more reliable when it comes from independent external sources, such as bank and customer confirmations, and when it is in original documents. Audit sampling lets the auditor draw conclusions about a whole population from a selection of items. Possible duplicate or unsupported transactions are tested by tracing them to invoices, orders and goods received notes.

## ISA 265 Communicating deficiencies in internal control
The auditor communicates significant deficiencies in internal control in writing to those charged with governance on a timely basis. Examples of weak controls include missing segregation of duties, missing approvals and bank reconciliations that nobody reviews.

## Pakistan: which framework applies
Which accounting and auditing framework applies depends on the type and size of the entity and on current rules from SECP and ICAP.
